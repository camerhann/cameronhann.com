UK flood risk is still sold as an outline. The 1-in-100. A polygon. A yes or a no.

That was a reasonable product when a model run cost a fortnight and a forecast was a warning symbol. It is a strange product now that we can ask a more useful question: given this storm, on this ground, what is the chance this building gets wet?

![Four-step chain: storm, CEFA hydrograph band, Tiamat depth map, probability a building floods](/images/articles/the-probabilistic-flood/01-handshake.jpg)

[CEFA](/articles/the-flood-is-a-shape) (Catchment-Event Frequency Analysis) turns rain and catchment into a hydrograph with a range. [Tiamat](/articles/tiamat) turns each hydrograph into a depth map. Stack the maps and each cell has a fraction: dry in the low member, wet in the high one. That fraction is a probability. It is not a mood. It is a count of physically computed floods.

## The missing object

British flood practice split the job years ago and never quite glued it back together.

The statistical pathway, FEH (the Flood Estimation Handbook), gives you a peak. How rare. How big.

The rainfall-runoff pathway gives you a hydrograph. Usually a tidy one.

The hydraulic model spreads that hydrograph across the land and draws a map.

Each step is competent. The join is the weak point. The peak does not know the volume. The triangle does not know this storm. The map does not know it was fed a cartoon. You can do everything "right" and still have the water in the wrong street, because the object that carries the storm into the map was never this river.

CEFA is the hydrograph as a property of the catchment, with a band instead of a pretence of certainty. Tiamat is physics fast enough to spread the band, not just the middle. The handshake is the flood.

## What is already online

The live public piece is the first half. [forecast.cameronhann.com](https://forecast.cameronhann.com) takes forecast rainfall for England and Wales and reports a CEFA peak range at the gauges. Experimental. Not a flood warning. For those, use the [Environment Agency service](https://check-for-flooding.service.gov.uk/).

The forecast will tell you roughly how big a river's response looks. It is silent on the exact minute of the peak. Magnitude first. Timing is the part that is easiest to oversell.

[hydrometric.io](https://hydrometric.io) is the same engine on the design desk: click a catchment, get a band and a characteristic hydrograph.

Neither site draws inundation yet. That is the next link.

## What goes up next

Tiamat forecasts will be run and put online. The thing I want on that page is not another 1-in-100 outline. It is this week's rain, turned into this week's chance.

A low hydrograph. A typical one. A high one. Three Tiamat maps. A cell that is dry in the first and wet in the third is not "the 1-in-100". It is a place the storm can reach, with a measured fraction of the ensemble behind it.

![Schematic of three Tiamat maps from the low, typical and high CEFA hydrographs](/images/articles/the-probabilistic-flood/02-ensemble.jpg)

That is a different product from a planning flood zone. A zone is a policy. A probability updates when the rain forecast does. Insurers already think that way. So do people who live on the wrong side of a line that was drawn for a different storm.

Two habits have to go for that to mean anything.

The first is the single hydrograph. There is not one. A peak of a given rarity can arrive as a short convective punch or a long winter soak. Those two floods do not cover the same houses. CEFA already ships a band. Tiamat should run the band, not the middle.

The second is the idea that a flood map is a photograph of a peak. A map is an integral. Volume, timing, and whether the floodplain had time to fill *are* the flood. That is why the [shape article](/articles/the-flood-is-a-shape) exists, and why [Tiamat](/articles/tiamat) is a physics engine rather than a lookup table.

## The future we actually hold

I am not claiming we now know the future. I am claiming we finally have the objects the future is made of: a storm, a catchment, a hydrograph with a range, and a model fast enough to spread it.

The 1-in-100 can stay as a planning language. The operational product is a probability, updated when the rain is.

That is the thing in our hands. The work is to put it on a map without lying about the edges.
